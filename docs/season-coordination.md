*Bao Thien Ngo · cPALSs Festivals Chair · July 2026*

Season coordination is **not season-specific**. Lunar New Year and Autumn both run crowded regional calendars — multiple hosts, overlapping weekends, shared vendors and volunteers. The practice is the same in every season: **assign lanes** instead of treating the list like a turf war.

Below is one organizer's read, using **Greater Sacramento autumn 2026** as the worked example. The same lane logic applies to [LNY](lny/market-research.html) and any other festival season.

**Live calendars:** [Autumn market landscape](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1Va0oCv09nyW98-JnWL9DuYYTsXG35cxjf_WqBZsySrA/edit) · [LNY market landscape](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1IB6beiKHC9R05r2o0imkh8MX_2u-qmhlVxTV3pxibs0/edit) · [Using market research](research.html)

## How it reads without intention

Look at that list with no coordination behind it and the obvious read is: **a bunch of events stepping on each other.**

- Two or three Mid-Autumn festivals in the same metro, some in the same city
- Vendors asked to book every weekend in September and October
- The same volunteers recruited for every flyer
- Organizers sizing up attendance claims and treating the calendar like a zero-sum fight

That's not paranoia. Food trucks won't eat losses twice. Volunteer cores burn out. Families pick one free Saturday and stay home the next. **Without lane coordination, the season looks like competition even when nobody wants a turf war.**

## What I'd change

I wouldn't try to merge everything into one mega-festival. I wouldn't chase the biggest headcount on a single weekend either.

**I'd coordinate the calendar into roles and markets that fit each host best** — then cross-promote across the gaps instead of fighting over the same product.

| Event | Lane / market | Role in the season |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|
| ASM Nguyen (Sep 12 est.) | Pocket / neighborhood park | Early warm-up — evening moon gathering the same Saturday as Riverside |
| Riverside UMC (Sep 12) | Parish / neighborhood | Warm-up — build a lantern, bring community together |
| CAAPS (Sep 19) | Spectacle / night market | Asian Fest + Music Fest @ Power Inn — different product than family park fair |
| Sac State Harvest Moon (Sep 20 est.) | Campus daytime | Courtyard / student fair — not the Elk Grove family evening product |
| California Is My Home (Sep 20) | Elk Grove producers | Parallel fair — vendors, stage, local press |
| APIDA Night Lights (Sep 21) | Campus / Gen Z evening | Heritage + mainstream on campus after the Sunday fairs |
| ARI (Sep 24) | Restaurant / community dinner | Intimate autumn moon meal — not a festival footprint |
| Great Lantern Festival | Elk Grove families *(paused)* | **Canceled for 2026** — was planned as family capstone; coalition diverting capacity to sister hosts |
| Giant Pumpkin Festival (Oct 3–4) | Civic / regional fall | October destination — pumpkin weigh-off; room for a lantern exhibition without a Derr-Okamoto weekend |
| SF Supermarket (Oct 3–4 est.) | Retail / plaza | Foot traffic — mooncakes, kits, lantern museum for entries the park can't store |

Same events. Different assignment: **who each one is for, what job it does, which weekend it owns.**

A parish fair isn't trying to be Sac State. CAAPS spectacle isn't trying to be the family fair at District 56. When each host knows its lane, cross-promo becomes honest — *Riverside parish + ASM park Sep 12 · Power Inn lights Sep 19 · CAIMH District 56 Sep 20 · campus Night Lights Sep 21 · ARI dinner Sep 24 · pumpkins + market plaza Oct 3–4* — instead of every flyer claiming to be "the only" or "the biggest." With Great Lantern Festival paused for 2026, that honesty matters even more: support the hosts who *are* running, don't invent a competing capstone.

## What actually matters

**Attendance numbers aren't the end-all-be-all.**

If vendors lose money, they don't come back. If the same ten people staff every event in the season, they don't come back either. A huge gate count one year means nothing if the ecosystem shrinks the next.

What I'd optimize for instead:

- **Sustainability** — financial (honest vendor math, right-sized fairs) and human (bounded roles, no burnout across September → November → February)
- **Lane coordination** — intentional splits so we're not duplicating the same product on the same weekend, and small events can feed a capstone instead of competing with it
- **Products that fulfill real individual needs** — families, campus, parish, spectacle seekers, and shoppers each get an honest fit; not one generic "festival" pretending to be everything

The line I keep coming back to: **If your vendors and core team can repeat next year, the season wins — regardless of who owns which weekend.**
